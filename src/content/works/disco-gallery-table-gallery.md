During his residency at the International Studio & Curatorial Program (ISCP) in New York in 2007, Fatmir Mustafa Karllo questioned the relationship between the artist, the curator, and the presentation of art.

For a scheduled meeting with a curator, the artist invited two street musicians from Gambia to perform in his place. The performance did not take place because the artist with whom he shared the studio objected, stating that people from the street could not be brought into their shared space. In response, he divided the studio with masking tape and declared his section an independent space called *Disco Gallery*.

In another meeting, he challenged curator Herb Tam to an arm-wrestling match in a space he called *Table Gallery*. The condition was simple: the curator could see his work only if he won. The artist won the match and presented no artworks, suggesting that the curator should practice harder so that next time he might deserve to see his work.
