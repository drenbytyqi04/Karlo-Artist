An Artwork emerges also as a need to better recognize the world around, as a kind of mutual request to be immersed in the reality created by simple stories, history quite simple to return to attempted confrontation: artist - reality, reality - history. In these “waters”, social contexts cross borders, rotates Fatmir Mustafa Karllo’s artist work installation “The Moon”.

Simple narrative realities but with a strong social intensity, where human drama passes through extremely thin thread history research. The artist owes us a personal commitment as an intruder in the history of human drama, being presented as a researcher of invisible details... he presents to the public an invisible world.

Installation “The Moon” comes from a long and direct process that the artist has done in the premises of a degraded former Metallurgical Factory in Elbasan, Albania. This huge complex (once with a greater glory) already totally degraded forgetfulness in time, after the fall of the Berlin Wall in 1990. As a ruined giant, this place still bears the silence of human drama and devotion.

“The Moon” comes from these surreal and absurd spaces, like a translucent labyrinth towards human figures. The artist directs this labyrinth to the sense of recognizing the invisible characters... people who were forgotten... by returning them, in being present, with the discovery of “effort and commitment”. Human commitment is also part of existential war.

Physical perception is an important element in this work, because it passes between the sense of smell. The artist often uses a bridge-crossing senses to perceive the work, return guests and interactive participants of his research, proposing at the same time new ways of perception of an artwork.

“The Moon” by artist Fatmir Mustafa Karllo is just “a bridge of sensations of smell” to deepen knowledge and complexity of simple reality, given that he tends to preserve exactly this smell like a direct gesture, ironic, unique and meaningful.

Text by Klod Dedja, 2014
