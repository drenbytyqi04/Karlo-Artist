The two neon letters “It’s Difficult” and “You Better Stop It” sound like familiar advice, well-meaning calls to retreat. They are typical crisis prevention phrases that put the brakes on a movement in order to avoid trouble. Their succinct conclusion sounds like general life advice. At the same time, they are the core sentences of a poetic commentary in which Karllo describes his experience as an artist with the “intellectualized and subtle” bureaucracy of high artistic elitism and administrative language conversations that have accompanied his work for years. The poems from which these sentences are taken were published in 2017 under the title “Today, Yesterday, Tomorrow” in “Rab-Rab: Journal for Political and Formal Investigations in Art”. In his poetic analysis of the language of the academic and market-oriented art world, Karllo says: “In these poems, I have exposed how this situation even gets into the skin of the language artists use when they present their work or talk about artistic concepts and ideas.”
