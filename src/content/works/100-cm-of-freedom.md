The beginning of the work 100 cm of Freedom is a declaration signed by the father of the artist Fatmir Mustafa Karllo. It entails the transfer of 100 cm of the lawn in his front garden to his son Karllo. For the work 100 cm of Freedom, this specifically means that this area will no longer be mown from 24 April 2026 onwards.

The surveillance camera continuously records the same segment of the garden. The image shifts from daylight to nighttime without interruption, keeping a 100 cm circle always centered within the frame.

## Curatorial Perspective by Wassili Widmer

“The work is the process” is a sentence Karllo repeatedly mentioned talking about his work 100 cm of Freedom. Pretty much right after agreeing on the collaboration with Vorgarten Domino, Karllo sent one of the most important steps of the process: a declaration from his father that there’s a 100 cm diameter area in his front garden that he doesn’t own and will no longer mow. According to Karllo, making this deal was hard diplomacy, as the father firstly didn’t like leaving grass unmown, and secondly would have preferred the back yard for it. Karllo mentions that his father doesn’t have the same affection for art as he himself does, a feeling probably many artists have come across with their parents. Through a lot of conversation and a bit of support from the mother, the two found a consensus, sealed with the written and signed declaration and a handshake. For Karllo, this handshake was one if not the most important emotional moment of the work, as it manifested a beautiful new moment of commonality with his father. The last paragraph of the declaration reads: “In return, Fatmir allows me [the father] to breathe the air of this area whenever I work in the other parts of the garden.”

Not only is there now a 100 cm diameter area in the front garden growing free, but also a bronze cast of Karllo’s hand in life size. The sculpture lies in the middle of the grass circle. In the final video, one can see a loop of a day of *100 cm of Freedom*. It is filmed over the course of 24 hours from a CCTV perspective. This was a conscious decision by Karllo. There might be some freedom in a private space, but in most of our political systems it’s the case that a higher entity is surveilling this freedom. Much of what is seen as freedom is rather a simulation, Karllo mentions. On the other hand, he also mentions that in the country of Kosovo there is a new sense of freedom since the declaration of independence in 2008. To the question of what the front garden means to Karllo, he answers that it’s an interesting example of a government, on a small scale and much more joyful. The human hand is taking a lot of impact, for example by cutting every blade of grass to the same height. By how it looks, it reveals how the house it belongs to looks on the inside.

The artwork creates a dynamic mix of the media sculpture, performance, video, environmental as well as political art. Karllo mentions a certain moment of his career when he realised that art doesn’t have to be a creative expression in one medium, like a painting or sculpture. From there on, he developed an individual freedom in creating his multi-layered artistic language. With great care for detail, he manages to create something in 100 cm of Freedom that is accessible through a strong visual and symbolic language, but at the same time never feels graspable in its full scale, as it works on so many artistic layers and temporalities. It doesn’t intend to focus on one simplified perspective of freedom, but rather includes thoughts and references that revolve around political, ecological and social topics. “The work is the process,” and seeing the whole scale of 100 cm of Freedom, one can clearly see that the artist has put a lot of thought into the topic of freedom while seemingly immediately progressing it. And this process won’t stop, as long as the grass is growing, below and around the hand, creating a wonderful symbiosis between a meaningful artistic object and the permanent progress of nature.
