## Frozen Screams

The building in the old part of Prizren appears pleasant and peaceful from the outside, but it carries a frightening shadow: its notorious place in the history of the suffering of the city’s citizens. It was once an investigation prison under the communist regime.

Today, it is a forgotten place, especially the basement, where the prisoners’ cells were located. Half a century ago, the investigation department moved to another, more modern building, and the house was turned into an archive. The former cells remained in darkness, visited only by a few people struggling with drug addiction.

When I entered some of the cells, apart from the dirt and cobwebs, the walls caught my attention. There were inscriptions, drawings, and strange scribbles, an archive of horror that had not seen the light of day for a long time. I experienced them as the screams of unknown people who had spent time in those tiny cells, measuring approximately 3.5 square meters, and who, in the darkness, had carved their names, the year of their imprisonment, some indecipherable signs, and even the notes of a melody...

Legends say that screams of torture could once be heard coming from that basement. Those who carved marks into the walls needed to communicate with the outside world and with the normal life from which they had been deprived. I was not interested in whether they had been political prisoners, patriots, traitors, or ordinary criminals. The medieval-like cells themselves, where one could barely take two or three steps, filled me with horror and took my breath away.

I felt the desire to bring them into the light exactly as they were, since for half a century they had longed for someone to see them as frozen screams.

I heard that the Municipality of Prizren was planning the reconstruction and restoration of the former investigation prison, including the renovation of the basement for a new purpose. My project aims to archive part of these walls as a tribute to human suffering…

Fatmir Mustafa Karllo
